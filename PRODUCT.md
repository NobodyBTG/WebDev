# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Small-town residents of all ages — retirees checking on a council meeting,
parents looking up a youth league schedule, renters paying a water bill from
their phone on a lunch break. They are not technical. They may be on slow
connections or older devices. They trust their town but are skeptical of
websites that feel corporate or distant. Clarity and approachability are
non-negotiable; anything that feels like a government portal from 2009 or a
big-city enterprise site is a miss.

## Product Purpose

A redesigned civic web platform for small American towns. Residents use it
to pay utility bills (water, trash), stay current on local events and
announcements, contact town departments, and find meeting minutes and
agendas. The design should make the community feel like the site belongs to
them — not to a software vendor.

## Brand Personality

Warm, rooted, straight-talking. Three words: **civic, alive, approachable**.
The tone is a trusted neighbor on the town council, not a press release.
Bold and playful where it earns it (events, community celebrations), clear
and confident where it matters (bills, emergencies, deadlines).

## Anti-references

- Generic municipal portal templates (dark navy + tiny text + 47 nav items)
- Enterprise SaaS "civic tech" that feels like it was built for a city of
  500,000, not a town of 4,000
- Cream / beige "warm and welcoming" AI defaults that read as dated
- Clipart-heavy community sites from the 2000s
- Overly playful designs that undermine trust for official functions

## Design Principles

1. **Legibility over decoration.** Residents come here to do something.
   Never let visual interest get in the way of finding a phone number,
   paying a bill, or reading a notice.
2. **Community-owned, not vendor-owned.** The design should feel local and
   specific, not like a white-labeled template. Concrete details over
   abstraction.
3. **Trust through clarity.** Government sites carry civic authority. Earn
   that trust with confident typography and clear hierarchy, not by hiding
   behind visual noise.
4. **Bold where it earns it.** Events, seasonal banners, and community
   milestones can carry energy and color. Utility functions (pay bill,
   contact, notice) stay calm and direct.
5. **Accessible by default.** Residents of all ages and abilities will use
   this. Contrast, text size, and keyboard navigation matter from day one.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA as the baseline. Assume a meaningful percentage of users are
over 55 and on mobile. Text must be readable at default browser sizes; no
"click to enlarge" micro-type. Touch targets must be comfortable.
